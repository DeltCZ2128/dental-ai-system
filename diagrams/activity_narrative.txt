NOTATION LEGEND

  Filled circle            Initial node - the activity begins.
  Circle inside a circle   Final node - the activity ends.
  Rounded rectangle        An action. Actions drawn in the left lane are
                           performed by the actor; actions drawn in the
                           right lane are performed by the proposed system.
  Diamond                  A decision point. Every outgoing branch of a
                           decision is labelled Yes or No.
  Red rounded rectangle    An alternative outcome placed beside its
                           decision. The flow re-enters the main path
                           after the outcome has been carried out.
  Arrowed line             The direction of the flow. A line that crosses
                           the vertical lane divider shows the hand-over
                           between the actor and the proposed system.
  Start / End              Text tags marking the initial and final nodes.

  The left lane of every figure is the lane of the actor of that role
  (Patient, Dentist or Administrator) and the right lane is always the
  Proposed System. The right lane additionally holds the spine of the
  diagram (the main sequence of system actions) while the outcome boxes
  hang to the right of their decisions.

FIGURE 8. ACTIVITY DIAGRAM - PATIENT ROLE

The patient figure contains nineteen actions, eleven decisions and six
alternative outcomes.

  1.  The activity begins at the initial node. The first decision asks
      whether the person is a first-time patient. A first-time patient
      fills in the registration form and submits it; the root register
      page forwards the request to the administrator registration module
      (register.php:3) and the new account record is inserted into the
      users table (admin/register.php:152). An existing patient skips
      this step and both paths meet at the login form.

  2.  The patient enters the username and password and clicks Log in.
      The system verifies the submitted credentials with
      password_hash verification (login.php:64). If the credentials are
      wrong the system displays the message Invalid email or password
      (login.php:97) and the flow returns to the login form, so the
      error outcome loops back rather than ending the activity.

  3.  When the credentials are valid the system opens the session
      (login.php:76) and redirects the browser to the patient dashboard
      according to the role of the account (login.php:83 to login.php:87).

  4.  From the dashboard the patient may click the Book Appointment
      button. The decision Book an appointment separates the patients
      who want to book from those who only want to manage an existing
      appointment; the No branch goes directly to My Appointments.

  5.  The Yes branch opens the booking form and the list of dental
      services (patient/book_appointment.php). The patient chooses a
      dental service, a dentist and an appointment date, and the system
      lists the available time slots for that date.

  6.  The decision Slots available handles an empty list. When no slot
      is free the system tells the patient that the dentist is not
      available on the chosen date and time
      (patient/book_appointment.php:1065, patient/book_appointment.php:898)
      and the flow returns to the service and date selection.


  7.  When slots exist the patient selects one and the decision Slot
      still free protects against a slot that was taken in the
      meantime. If the slot is already booked the system shows the
      message that asks the patient to choose another time
      (patient/book_appointment.php:985) and again returns to the
      service and date selection.

  8.  The patient reviews the appointment summary and the system saves
      the appointment: the record is inserted (INSERT at
      patient/book_appointment.php:1130) with the status Pending
      (patient/book_appointment.php:1149). The confirmation message is
      shown and the My Appointments page lists the bookings.

  9.  In My Appointments the patient selects a record and the decision
      Manage this appointment starts the management cascade. Choosing
      No ends the session at the logout action.

 10.  Choosing Yes opens the action menu of the appointment. The
      decision Confirm attendance lets the patient confirm a visit; the
      confirmation action and its notification are written by
      patient/my_appointments.php:41 and patient/my_appointments.php:61.
      The decision Request reschedule lets the patient ask the clinic to
      move the visit (patient/my_appointments.php:69,
      patient/my_appointments.php:99) and the decision Cancel
      appointment sets the status to Cancelled
      (patient/my_appointments.php:130, patient/my_appointments.php:166)
      with a notification to the clinic
      (patient/my_appointments.php:176). These three outcomes are drawn
      beside their decisions and all three merge into the action that
      displays the updated appointment status on the dashboard.

 11.  The remaining decisions continue down the cascade. Raise a
      concern? sends the typed message to the clinic, which inserts the
      record (patient/appointment_concern.php:422). Update profile?
      saves the edited personal information (patient/profile.php:267);
      the same page also stores a changed password
      (patient/profile.php:371, patient/profile.php:379). A No answer on
      either decision moves the flow forward without an outcome box.

 12.  Every path converges on Log out of the system and the activity
      ends at the final node.


FIGURE 9. ACTIVITY DIAGRAM - DENTIST ROLE

The dentist figure contains fifteen actions, five decisions and two
alternative outcomes.

  1.  The activity begins with the same login behaviour as Figure 8:
      credential verification (login.php:64), the invalid credentials
      message on failure (login.php:97), the session on success
      (login.php:76) and the redirect that depends on the role
      (login.php:83 to login.php:87). The failed login is drawn as the
      first outcome box and loops back to the login form.

  2.  The system opens the dentist dashboard with the schedule of the
      day (dentist/dentist_dashboard.php:14). From there the dentist
      checks the list of patients assigned for today
      (dentist/dentist_dashboard.php:14) and opens the patient queue
      page (dentist/queue.php), where the system shows the patients who
      are still waiting, counted by status
      (dentist/queue.php:311, dentist/queue.php:335).

  3.  The decision Treat the next patient keeps the dentist in the
      queue view (No) until a patient is taken; the second outcome box
      therefore reports that the queue is empty. The Yes branch starts
      the treatment of the selected patient
      (dentist/queue.php:1485), which moves the queue entry to the
      serving state. The allowed values Waiting, Serving and Completed
      are enforced before that update
      (dentist/dentist_dashboard.php:65).

  4.  With the entry serving, the dentist reviews the patient record
      and the appointment history of that patient
      (dentist/patients.php:467, dentist/patients.php:740) and performs
      the dental consultation and treatment.

  5.  The decision Change the appointment status asks whether the visit
      should be closed. The Yes branch sets the appointment of the
      patient to Completed (dentist/dentist_dashboard.php:96); the same
      whitelist also permits Pending, Confirmed and Cancelled
      (dentist/dentist_dashboard.php:90). The No branch skips the
      change. Both branches then set the queue entry to Completed, so
      an entry never remains in the serving state, and the flow
      continues to the notification inbox.

  6.  The decision Check the notification inbox? opens the list of
      notifications of the dentist (dentist/notifications.php:164) and
      reads the messages; a No answer steps over the inbox. The two
      branches meet at the decision Update your profile?

  7.  The decision Update your profile? opens the profile page, where
      the dentist edits the personal information and saves the changes
      (dentist/profile.php:463, dentist/profile.php:534). A No answer
      leaves the profile step out.

  8.  Every path converges on Log out of the system and the activity
      ends at the final node.



FIGURE 10. ACTIVITY DIAGRAM - ADMINISTRATOR ROLE (PART 1)

Figure 10 and Figure 11 together describe one role. To keep every flow
readable the administrator diagram is printed on two parts, with the
part numbers continuous across the two parts: Figure 10 carries login,
registration, scheduling and the daily queue, and Figure 11 carries the
dentist, settings and reporting modules. Figure 10 contains seventeen
actions, eleven decisions and three alternative outcomes.

  1.  The activity begins with the same login behaviour as Figures 8
      and 9 (login.php:64, login.php:97, login.php:76,
      login.php:83 to login.php:87). The system then opens the
      administrator dashboard and the appointment management page
      (admin/appointments.php).

  2.  The decision Register a new patient? opens the registration
      form. When the administrator submits the record the new account
      is inserted into the users table (admin/register.php:152); a No
      answer skips the form. Both branches meet at the decision
      Schedule an appointment?

  3.  The decision Schedule an appointment? lets the administrator
      choose the patient, the service, the dentist, the date and the
      time. The system then checks the availability of the dentist for
      that slot: the conflict test (admin/appointments.php:26) produces
      the message that a scheduling conflict was detected
      (admin/appointments.php:36).

  4.  The decision Is a slot free? separates the two outcomes of that
      check. When the slot is free the system saves the appointment
      with the status Pending (admin/appointments.php:61 and
      admin/appointments.php:72). When it is not, the outcome box tells
      the administrator that the slot is not free and the flow returns
      to the appointment form.

  5.  The decision Change the appointment status? moves a stored
      appointment to Confirmed, Completed or Cancelled; the permitted
      values are checked against the whitelist before the update
      (admin/appointments.php:101). A No answer steps over the step.

  6.  The decision Send an appointment reminder? sends the reminder to
      the patient (admin/appointments.php:161) and the decision
      Reschedule an appointment? accepts the new date and time
      (admin/appointments.php:238). The reschedule runs the same
      conflict test again (admin/appointments.php:259), and the
      decision Is the new slot available? separates a free slot from
      the outcome box that reports the slot is not available, which
      returns the flow to the appointment form.

  7.  The decision Manage the walk-in queue? opens the QR walk-in
      form (admin/queue.php:24). The system creates the walk-in
      patient number WALK-... (admin/queue.php:38) and assigns the
      queue number of the day (admin/queue.php:43). A direct entry
      takes the same path through the add action (admin/queue.php:78).

  8.  The decision Change the status of a queue entry? moves an entry
      through Waiting, Serving and Completed; the values are validated
      before the update (admin/queue.php:141). A No answer skips the
      change.

  9.  The decision Mark an appointment as No-Show? sets the status of
      a missed appointment to No-Show (admin/no_show.php:13) and
      notifies the patient. A No answer leaves the block.

 10.  Every path converges on the End tag. The activity continues in
      Figure 11, which resumes from the administrator dashboard.



FIGURE 11. ACTIVITY DIAGRAM - ADMINISTRATOR ROLE (PART 2)

Figure 11 is the second part of the administrator role. It contains
twenty-five actions, ten decisions and three alternative outcomes.

  1.  The activity resumes at the administrator dashboard. The decision
      Manage the dentists? opens the dentist management page, where the
      seven operations of that module are carried out in turn: add a
      dentist (admin/dentists.php:24), edit a dentist
      (admin/dentists.php:49), activate or deactivate the account
      (admin/dentists.php:69), set the weekly schedule
      (admin/dentists.php:83), delete a schedule slot
      (admin/dentists.php:117), register a day-off
      (admin/dentists.php:127) and remove a day-off
      (admin/dentists.php:158). A No answer skips the whole block.

  2.  The decision Edit a patient record? opens the patient form and
      saves the corrected details (admin/patients.php); a No answer
      steps over it.

  3.  The decision Open the patient concern inbox? lists the concerns
      that have not been answered (admin/appointment_concern.php).
      Inside that block the decision Mark all the remaining concerns as
      read? either clears the rest of the inbox in one action
      (admin/appointment_concern.php:94) or goes straight on. The
      system selects a concern and marks it as read
      (admin/appointment_concern.php:43), the administrator types the
      reply and sends it to the patient, and the system shows the
      confirmation that the reply was sent. A No answer on the inbox
      decision bypasses the whole block through the right-hand channel.

  4.  The decision Change the clinic settings? opens the settings form.
      The decision Settings valid? then separates a valid submission,
      which is written to the settings table (admin/settings.php:22),
      from an invalid one, which shows the invalid settings message
      (admin/settings.php:28) and returns to the form. A No answer on
      Change the clinic settings? skips the block through the
      right-hand channel.

  5.  The decision Manage the dental services or the user accounts?
      covers the remaining settings operations: a dental service is
      added, edited or disabled (admin/settings.php:44,
      admin/settings.php:64) and a user account of the clinic is added
      or edited (admin/settings.php:70). A No answer skips the block.

  6.  The decision View the reports and the demand forecast? opens the
      reports page, where the headline figures of the clinic are
      counted (admin/reports.php:34, admin/reports.php:35) together
      with the appointments of each dentist (admin/reports.php:76).
      The system then trains a Linear Regression model on the
      historical demand (admin/demand_forecast.php:51) and predicts the
      appointment demand for the next seven days
      (admin/demand_forecast.php:393).

  7.  Two decisions classify the prediction against the historical
      average. A value at or above 125 per cent of the average marks
      the day as a high-demand day; a value at or above 75 per cent
      marks it as a medium-demand day; below 75 per cent marks it as a
      low-demand day (admin/demand_forecast.php:315,
      admin/demand_forecast.php:317). All three outcomes meet at the
      action that shows the day with the highest expected demand
      (admin/demand_forecast.php:394).

  8.  Every path converges on Log out of the system and the activity
      ends at the final node.

RELATIONSHIP TO THE OTHER DIAGRAMS

  Figures 8 to 11 describe the behaviour of the three roles of the
  proposed system, while Figure 12 describes the data that these
  activities read and write. The registration actions of Figures 8 and
  10 insert into the users and patients entities of the ERD, the
  booking actions of Figures 8 and 10 insert into the appointments
  entity, the queue actions of Figures 9, 10 and 11 write the queue
  entity, and the concern, notification and settings actions write the
  corresponding entities of the same diagram. The valid values shown as
  branch outcomes (Pending, Confirmed, Completed, Cancelled, No-Show,
  Waiting, Serving and Completed) are the values documented for the
  status columns in the Data Dictionary in Section 3.9.
