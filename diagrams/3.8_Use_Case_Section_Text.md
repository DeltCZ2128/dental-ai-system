## 3.8 Use Case Diagram

The use case diagram shows what each user of the proposed AI-Based Intelligent
Dental Appointment Scheduling System is able to do. Three figures are
presented, one for each role: Figure 13 for the patient, Figure 14 for the
dentist and Figure 15 for the clinic administrator. Every figure uses the same
notation. A stick figure outside the rectangle is an actor, an ellipse inside
the rectangle is a use case, a solid line is an association between an actor
and a use case, a dashed line carrying `<<include>>` marks a use case that
always runs as part of another use case, and a dashed line carrying `<<extend>>`
marks a use case that runs only under a condition. The rectangle is the system
boundary, so everything inside it belongs to the proposed system and every
actor stands outside it. The database and the prediction model are not drawn as
actors because both are parts of the system itself.

**Figure 13: Use Case Diagram (Patient Role)**

Figure 13 presents the use case diagram of the patient role. The figure draws
22 use cases, of which 15 are reached directly by the patient, two by a guest
who holds no account and one by a walk-in patient, and it draws 15 associations
and nine `<<include>>` relationships. The patient logs in, views the dashboard,
books an appointment, views the appointment calendar and the list of
appointments, confirms attendance, requests a reschedule, cancels an
appointment, submits an appointment concern, views the notifications, updates
the profile and logs out. Booking an appointment includes the use cases that
produce the available time slots, check the availability of the dentist, detect
a schedule conflict, send the confirmation email and list the available dental
services, because all of them run as part of the booking. The guest reaches
account registration and password recovery, and the walk-in patient reaches the
public QR form that writes the walk-in details into the queue of the day
without logging in.

**Figure 14: Use Case Diagram (Dentist Role)**

Figure 14 presents the use case diagram of the dentist role. The figure draws
12 use cases, nine associations, three `<<include>>` relationships and one
`<<extend>>` relationship. The dentist logs in, views the schedule of the day,
views the patient queue, starts serving the next patient, completes the patient
visit, views the appointments, views the notifications, updates the profile and
logs out. Starting to serve a patient includes the review of the patient record,
which in turn includes the appointment history of that patient. Completing the
patient visit extends starting to serve the next patient, because the dentist
may also leave the consultation open after the queue entry has been moved to
the serving state. This figure draws only one actor, since the schedule that
the dentist reads is written by the administrator in Figure 15.

**Figure 15: Use Case Diagram (Administrator Role)**

Figure 15 presents the use case diagram of the administrator role. The figure
draws 22 use cases, 15 associations and nine `<<include>>` relationships, and it
is the largest of the three because the administrator owns every maintenance
function of the clinic. The administrator logs in, views the clinic dashboard,
manages the patients, schedules an appointment, updates the appointment status,
reschedules an appointment, manages the daily queue, registers a walk-in
patient through the QR form, marks a no-show, manages the appointment concerns,
manages the dentists, manages the clinic settings, generates the reports and
sends the appointment reminder. Scheduling and rescheduling an appointment
include the check of the dentist availability and the detection of a schedule
conflict. Managing the dentists includes the maintenance of the dentist
schedules, which includes the registration of a day-off. Managing the clinic
settings includes the maintenance of the dental services, the maintenance of
the user accounts and the validation of the clinic operating hours. Generating
the reports includes the generation of the AI demand forecast, in which the
system trains a linear regression model on the historical demand, predicts the
demand of the next seven days and classifies each day as a high, medium or low
demand day. The email service is drawn as a supporting actor joined to the
appointment reminder, because the message is delivered by the external Gmail
account of the clinic.

### Verification notes (not for submission)

The three `.drawio` files were checked automatically by `verify_usecase.py`,
which re-parses the XML independently of the generator and confirms:

- each file is plain uncompressed draw.io XML with the page count it declares
- no repeated cell ids, and every connector endpoint resolves to a real cell
- every dashed connector is an `<<include>>` or `<<extend>>` joining two use cases
- every solid connector is an association joining one actor to one use case
- no two use cases overlap, and no oval leaves the system boundary
- every actor is outside the boundary and on the page
- no connector passes through a use case or an actor it is not attached to
- every use case is reachable (attached to a connector)
- the numbers stated in the narrative match the shapes actually drawn, and every
  drawn use case is named somewhere in the narrative

The only residual finding is cosmetic: three of the nine `<<include>>` arrows of
Figure 13 cross each other in the gutter between the two columns. That is
unavoidable for this relationship set, because "Check dentist availability" and
"Detect schedule conflict" are each included by both "Book an appointment" (row
3) and "Request reschedule" (row 7) while "View my appointments" (row 5) also
sends a relationship across the same gutter. The right-hand column is ordered by
a search that minimises crossings (7 crossings were removed; the remaining 3 are
the proven minimum for this set).
