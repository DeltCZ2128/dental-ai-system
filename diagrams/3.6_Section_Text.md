## 3.6 Activity Diagram

The activity diagrams in this section describe the sequence of activities
performed by each user of the proposed AI-Based Intelligent Dental
Appointment Scheduling System. Four swimlane figures are presented. Figure 7
shows the appointment-scheduling process as a whole, while Figures 8 to 11
show the behaviour of the three roles of the system taken one at a time: the
patient, the dentist and the administrator.

Every figure uses the same notation. The left lane belongs to the actor of
that role and the right lane always belongs to the proposed system, so an
arrow that crosses the vertical divider is the point at which the task is
handed over from the user to the system. A rounded rectangle is an action, a
diamond is a decision whose outgoing branches are always labelled Yes or No,
and a red rounded rectangle is an alternative outcome that re-enters the main
path once it has been carried out. Because the administrator role carries
eight separate sub-modules, its diagram is printed on two parts, Figure 10 and
Figure 11, and the part numbers are continuous across the two parts.

**Figure 7: Activity Diagram (Appointment Scheduling)**

The Activity Diagram describes the sequence of activities performed during
appointment scheduling.

The process begins when the user logs into the system. After authentication,
the user accesses the appointment module. The user selects the dentist,
service, date, and available time.

The system validates the information by checking the selected date, dentist
availability, dentist schedule, dentist rest day, specific day-off, service
duration, and possible appointment conflicts.

If the selected schedule is unavailable or conflicting, the system displays an
error or requests another schedule. If the schedule is available, the
appointment is confirmed and the record is updated.

**Figure 8: Activity Diagram (Patient Role)**

Figure 8 presents the activity diagram of the patient role. The patient
decides whether to register a first-time account, logs in with a username and
password, and is redirected to the patient dashboard. From the dashboard the
patient may book a new appointment or manage an existing one. During booking
the system lists the available time slots for the chosen date, checks once
more that the selected slot is still free, and saves the appointment with the
status Pending. In the management branch the patient can confirm attendance,
request a reschedule, cancel, raise a concern, or update the personal
profile, and the system reports the resulting appointment status on the
dashboard. The activity ends when the patient logs out.


**Figure 9: Activity Diagram (Dentist Role)**

Figure 9 presents the activity diagram of the dentist role. The dentist logs
in and opens the dashboard for the schedule of the day, checks the list of
patients assigned for today, and opens the patient queue page where the
patients still waiting are shown. Starting the treatment of the next patient
moves the queue entry to the serving state. The dentist then reviews the
patient record and the appointment history, performs the consultation, and
decides whether to close the visit; closing it sets the appointment to
Completed. In both cases the queue entry is then set to Completed so that no
entry is left in the serving state. The dentist may read the notification
inbox and update the profile before logging out. If no patient is waiting,
the system reports that the queue is empty and the dentist remains in the
queue view.

**Figure 10: Activity Diagram (Administrator Role, Part 1)**

Figure 10 presents the first part of the activity diagram of the
administrator role. The administrator logs in and opens the appointment
management page, where a new patient account may be registered. Scheduling an
appointment makes the system test the availability of the dentist for the
chosen slot; a free slot is saved with the status Pending, while an occupied
slot is reported and the form is shown again. The administrator can also
change the status of an appointment, send a reminder to the patient, or
reschedule it, in which case the availability test runs again against the new
date and time. The remaining decisions of this part cover the daily queue:
the walk-in QR form issues the walk-in patient number and the queue number of
the day, a queue entry can be moved through Waiting, Serving and Completed,
and a missed appointment can be marked No-Show.

**Figure 11: Activity Diagram (Administrator Role, Part 2)**

Figure 11 presents the second part of the activity diagram of the
administrator role. The administrator may manage the dentists, which covers
adding and editing a dentist, activating or deactivating the account,
maintaining the weekly schedule and registering day-offs, and may edit a
patient record. The concern inbox allows a concern to be marked as read
individually or in one action, after which the administrator types the reply
that is sent to the patient. The settings block validates the clinic
operating hours before they are written, and also maintains the list of
dental services and the user accounts of the clinic. Finally, the
administrator opens the reports page, where the system trains a Linear
Regression model on the historical demand of the clinic, predicts the demand
for the next seven days, classifies each day as a high, medium or low demand
day against 125 and 75 per cent of the historical average, and shows the day
with the highest expected demand. The activity ends when the administrator
logs out.
