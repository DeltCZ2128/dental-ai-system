NOTATION LEGEND
  Actor               A stick figure drawn outside the system boundary. It is a
                      person or an external service that reaches a function of
                      the system. The primary actor of each figure is drawn at
                      full size; a supporting actor is drawn smaller and in
                      grey. The database and the prediction model are not
                      actors, because they are parts of the system itself.

  Use case            An ellipse inside the system boundary. It is one complete
                      function that an actor can carry out, and it always
                      carries a verb phrase, for example "Book an appointment".

  Association         A plain solid line joining an actor to every use case that
                      the actor may carry out. It carries no arrowhead because
                      the exchange is two way.

  <<include>>         A dashed line with an open arrowhead pointing at the
                      included use case. The included function always runs as
                      part of the base function, so it is not optional. For
                      example, "Book an appointment" always includes "Check
                      dentist availability".

  <<extend>>          A dashed line with an open arrowhead pointing at the base
                      use case. The extending function runs only under a
                      condition that the base function does not require. For
                      example, "Complete patient visit" extends "Start serving
                      next patient" because the visit may be left open.

  System boundary     The plain rectangle on each page. Everything inside it
                      belongs to the proposed system; every actor stands
                      outside it. Its four sides carry no label, following the
                      sample figure supplied by the researcher.

ACTORS AND THEIR ACCESS LEVELS
  The system recognises three roles and two supporting parties.

  1. Patient. A registered account holder. The role is written to the session
     by login.php:76 and the dashboard is routed by login.php:86-89. The
     patient reaches 15 of the 22 use cases of Figure 13.

  2. Dentist. A clinician account. Routed by login.php:88-89. The dentist
     reaches 9 of the 12 use cases of Figure 14, which is the smallest of the
     three figures because the dentist reads the schedule that the
     administrator maintains.

  3. Clinic Administrator. Routed by login.php:84-85. The administrator
     reaches 14 of the 22 use cases of Figure 15, including the clinic
     settings, the dentist records and the reports with the AI forecast.

  4. Guest. A visitor who holds no account. The guest reaches only "Register
     account" (register.php) and "Recover password" (forgot_password.php with
     reset_password.php). This actor is what closes the entry point of the
     diagram: without it, "Log in" would have no way of coming into existence.

  5. Walk-in Patient. A patient who arrives at the clinic without an account
     and without logging in. The public page walkin_qr.php lets this actor
     reach "Submit walk-in details (QR)", which writes the queue row that the
     administrator later serves. This actor is drawn in Figure 13 only.

  6. Email Service. The external Gmail account that delivers the messages of
     the clinic, implemented as sendClinicGmail() in config.php. It is drawn in
     Figure 15 because the reminder is handed over to it by the administrator.

  The database (users, patients, dentists, appointments, queue,
  appointment_concerns, notifications, clinic_services and the rest of
  dental_ai.sql) is deliberately not drawn as an actor, and neither is the
  prediction model, because both live inside the boundary.


FIGURE 13. USE CASE DIAGRAM - PATIENT ROLE
  The patient figure contains 22 use cases, 15 associations and 9 <<include>>
  relationships. Three actors are drawn: the patient at full size, and the
  guest and the walk-in patient as smaller grey actors. The left column holds
  the use cases that an actor starts directly; the right column holds the use
  cases that are reached through an <<include>> relationship, so an arrow
  always points into them.

  1. Log in. The patient submits the username and the password at login.php:29
     and the credentials are tested against the users table with
     password_verify() at login.php:64 before the session is created at
     login.php:73-76 and the browser is sent to patient_dashboard.php at
     login.php:86-87.

  2. View patient dashboard. patient_dashboard.php shows the next appointment,
     the appointment counters and the action menu of the patient.

  3. Book an appointment. patient/book_appointment.php handles
     action=book_appointment with the fields dentist_id, service_type,
     appointment_date, start_time, end_time and notes, and saves the row with
     the status Pending.

  4. View appointment calendar. patient/calendar.php presents the appointments
     of the patient on a month grid.

  5. View my appointments. patient/my_appointments.php lists every appointment
     of the patient together with its status, and holds the three actions that
     follow.

  6. Confirm attendance. my_appointments.php accepts action=confirm_attendance
     only while the appointment is still Pending or Confirmed, because the
     status is checked at my_appointments.php:52 and a past appointment is
     refused at my_appointments.php:55.

  7. Request reschedule. my_appointments.php accepts action=request_reschedule
     with the fields preferred_date and preferred_time, and the reason written
     to reschedule_reason. The request is stored as an appointment concern with
     the status New at my_appointments.php:95.

  8. Cancel appointment. my_appointments.php accepts action=cancel_appointment
     and stores the reason given in cancel_notes, after which the appointment
     carries the status Cancelled.

  9. Submit appointment concern. patient/appointment_concern.php accepts the
     fields appointment_id, reason and message and inserts the row into the
     appointment_concerns table, which the administrator answers in Figure 15.

  10. View notifications. patient/notifications.php lists the reminders, the
     confirmations and the automatic no-show notices that config.php:246
     raises, and records the opening of a reminder through log_email_click.

  11. Update profile. patient/profile.php handles action=update_profile with
     first_name, last_name, birth_date, gender, contact_number, address and
     email; the same page carries action=change_password.

  12. Log out. logout.php clears the session and returns the browser to the
     login page.

  13. Register account. Reachable by the guest actor. register.php creates the
     users row and the matching patients row with a generated patient number
     from functions.php:75, and it is the only way an account comes into
     existence apart from the administrator in Figure 15.

  14. Recover password. Reachable by the guest actor. forgot_password.php
     requests the reset and reset_password.php writes the new value.

  15. Submit walk-in details (QR). Reachable by the walk-in patient actor
     without any login. walkin_qr.php accepts the name, the contact number and
     the service, and inserts the row that gives the patient number, the
     walk-in number and the queue number of the day.

  The nine <<include>> relationships of Figure 13 are the following.

  1. Book an appointment includes View available time slots, because the free
     slots of the chosen dentist and date are produced by
     patient/get_available_slots.php before the form can be submitted.

  2. Book an appointment includes Check dentist availability, which tests the
     dentist against the requested date and time.

  3. Book an appointment includes Detect schedule conflict, which tests the
     weekly schedule of the dentist, the rest day, the clinic day-off and the
     service duration against the requested slot and rejects the booking when
     the slot is already taken.

  4. Request reschedule includes Check dentist availability, because the new
     slot is tested again.

  5. Request reschedule includes Detect schedule conflict, for the same reason
     as relationship 3.

  6. Book an appointment includes Send confirmation email, which is the hand
     over to sendClinicGmail() in config.php.

  7. Book an appointment includes View available dental services, because the
     form is filled from the clinic_services table at
     book_appointment.php:517.

  8. View my appointments includes View appointment history, which is the past
     appointment list that the same page renders.

  9. Update profile includes Change password, because the password form is
     handled by the same page through action=change_password.

  No <<extend>> relationship is drawn in Figure 13: every listed function runs
  whenever its base function runs, and none of them is conditional.

FIGURE 14. USE CASE DIAGRAM - DENTIST ROLE
  The dentist figure contains 12 use cases, 9 associations, 3 <<include>>
  relationships and 1 <<extend>> relationship. Only one actor is drawn, the
  dentist, because the clinician does not hand any function over to a second
  party: the schedule that is read here is written by the administrator in
  Figure 15. This is the smallest of the three figures.

  1. Log in. The dentist is routed to dentist_dashboard.php by login.php:88-89.

  2. View today's schedule. dentist/dentist_dashboard.php lists the
     appointments assigned to the dentist for the current day from the
     appointments table, and posts action together with appointment_id,
     queue_id and status to move an appointment forward.

  3. View patient queue. dentist/queue.php lists the queue of the day and
     filters it by the status through the filter parameter, so the dentist may
     look at the All, Waiting, Serving or Completed entries.

  4. Start serving next patient. dentist/queue.php posts queue_id together
     with status=Serving, which moves the next waiting entry into the serving
     state.

  5. Complete patient visit. dentist/queue.php posts the closing status and
     dentist/dentist_dashboard.php posts status as well, which sets the
     appointment to Completed and the queue entry to Completed.

  6. View appointments. dentist/appointments.php lists the appointments of the
     dentist and allows a single appointment to be opened through the view
     parameter.

  7. View notifications. dentist/notifications.php lists the appointment
     activity for the dentist and accepts the filters All, New, Upcoming and
     Cancelled.

  8. Update profile. dentist/profile.php writes full_name, specialization and
     contact_number.

  9. Log out. logout.php ends the session.

  10. Review patient record. Reached through an <<include>> relationship, never
      started on its own. dentist/patients.php shows the patient information
      and the medical record for the patient_id that the dentist asked for.

  11. View patient appointment history. Reached through an <<include>>
      relationship. The same page renders the appointment history that belongs
      to the patient record.

  12. Change password. Reached through an <<include>> relationship from Update
      profile, and handled by dentist/settings.php through
      action=change_password with current_password, new_password and
      confirm_password.

  The relationships of Figure 14 are the following.

  1. Start serving next patient includes Review patient record, because the
     record is opened for the patient who is being served, exactly as the
     activity diagram of Figure 9 draws it.

  2. Review patient record includes View patient appointment history, so the
     dentist sees the earlier visits while reading the record.

  3. Update profile includes Change password.

  4. Complete patient visit extends Start serving next patient. The arrowhead
     points at Start serving next patient because the visit may also be left
     open: the dentist may close the consultation, in which case the
     appointment becomes Completed, or may stop at the queue entry, in which
     case the appointment keeps its status. This condition is exactly the
     "Close the visit?" decision of Figure 9.

FIGURE 15. USE CASE DIAGRAM - ADMINISTRATOR ROLE
  The administrator figure contains 22 use cases, 15 associations and 9
  <<include>> relationships. Two actors are drawn: the clinic administrator at
  full size and the email service as a smaller grey actor. The figure is the
  largest of the three because the administrator owns every maintenance
  function of the clinic.

  1. Log in. The administrator is routed to admin/dashboard.php by
     login.php:84-85, and admin/includes.php builds the navigation of every
     administrator page.

  2. View clinic dashboard. admin/dashboard.php shows the clinic overview,
     including the buttons that open the forecast view.

  3. Manage patients. admin/patients.php writes first_name, middle_name,
     last_name, contact_number and email for a patient_id, and also creates the
     account of a walk-in patient who has no username yet.

  4. Schedule an appointment. admin/appointments.php handles action=add with
     patient_id, dentist_id, service_type, appointment_date, start_time,
     end_time and notes.

  5. Update appointment status. admin/appointments.php handles action=status
     with id and status, which is how Pending becomes Confirmed or Completed
     and how a cancellation is recorded.

  6. Reschedule appointment. admin/appointments.php handles action=reschedule
     and rechecks the new date and time before it is written.

  7. Manage daily queue. admin/queue.php handles action=status with id and
     status and moves an entry through Waiting, Serving and Completed; the
     priority and the is_senior flag are written by the same page.

  8. Register walk-in patient (QR). admin/queue.php handles action=qr_add with
     qr_name, qr_service and qr_priority and prints the public form. The page
     walkin_qr.php that the walk-in patient uses in Figure 13 is the other half
     of this function.

  9. Mark no-show. admin/no_show.php handles appointment_id and action. The
     status is also set without a click, because config.php:207-237 marks an
     appointment that was never served as No-Show and config.php:246-268 sends
     the notice to the patient.

  10. Manage appointment concerns. admin/appointment_concern.php lists the
      concerns that the patients submitted, marks them as read through
      notification_id, and stores the reply that is sent back.

  11. Manage dentists. admin/dentists.php handles action=add and action=edit
      with dentist_name, specialization and contact_number, and
      action=toggle_status to activate or deactivate an account.

  12. Manage clinic settings. admin/settings.php handles action=clinic with
      clinic_name, address, contact_number, email, opening_time and
      closing_time.

  13. Generate reports. admin/reports.php builds the appointment analytics and
      accepts the period parameter, and it is where the demand forecast is
      displayed together with the high-demand and the low-demand days at
      admin/reports.php:302-306.

  14. Send appointment reminder. admin/appointments.php handles action=remind,
      and the message is delivered by the external Gmail account
      sendClinicGmail() in config.php. This is the only use case of the three
      figures that is shared with a supporting actor, so the email service is
      joined to it as well as the administrator.

  The nine <<include>> relationships of Figure 15 are the following.

  1. Schedule an appointment includes Check dentist availability.

  2. Reschedule appointment includes Check dentist availability, because the
     new slot is tested in the same way.

  3. Schedule an appointment includes Detect schedule conflict, which tests the
     dentist schedule, the rest day, the clinic day-off and the service
     duration, exactly as the patient side does in Figure 13.

  4. Manage dentists includes Manage dentist schedules, which admin/dentists.php
     handles through action=schedule with schedule_date, start_time and
     end_time and through action=delete_schedule with schedule_id.

  5. Manage dentist schedules includes Register day-off, handled by
     action=dayoff_add with off_date, rest_day and reason and by
     action=dayoff_delete with dayoff_id.

  6. Manage clinic settings includes Manage dental services, handled by
     action=service_save and action=service_status with service_id,
     service_name, description and duration_minutes; the active rows of
     clinic_services are the services that the patient books in Figure 13.

  7. Manage clinic settings includes Manage user accounts, handled by
     action=user_save with username, password and role.

  8. Manage clinic settings includes Validate clinic operating hours, because
     the opening time and the closing time are tested before they are written
     and the clinic_business_hours rows are kept consistent with them.

  9. Generate reports includes Generate AI demand forecast, which
     admin/demand_forecast.php performs: the historical demand is read, the
     straight line that fits it is computed by linearRegression() at
     admin/demand_forecast.php:51 and applied at line 246, the demand of the
     next seven days is predicted, and each day is classified as a high, medium
     or low demand day against 125 and 75 per cent of the historical average.
     The model is not drawn as an actor because it runs inside the boundary.

TRACEABILITY TO THE IMPLEMENTATION
  Every use case of the three figures is traceable to a page or to a handler of
  the delivered system, which is what makes the diagrams verifiable rather than
  aspirational. The mapping is the following.

  1. Authentication. Log in, Register account, Recover password and Log out map
     to login.php, register.php, forgot_password.php, reset_password.php and
     logout.php. The role is read from the users table and written to the
     session at login.php:73-76.

  2. Appointment booking. Book an appointment, View available time slots and
     View available dental services map to patient/book_appointment.php,
     patient/book_scheduling.php, patient/get_available_slots.php and the
     clinic_services table.

  3. Appointment management. View my appointments, Confirm attendance, Request
     reschedule, Cancel appointment and View appointment history map to
     patient/my_appointments.php, which is also the page that raises the
     appointment concern of a reschedule request at line 95.

  4. Clinical queue. View patient queue, Start serving next patient and Complete
     patient visit map to dentist/queue.php and to the queue table, whose status
     column holds Waiting, Serving and Completed.

  5. Clinic maintenance. Manage patients, Manage dentists, Manage dentist
     schedules, Register day-off, Manage dental services, Manage user accounts
     and Manage clinic settings map to admin/patients.php, admin/dentists.php
     and admin/settings.php.

  6. Concerns and notifications. Submit appointment concern, Manage appointment
     concerns, View notifications, Mark no-show and Send appointment reminder
     map to the appointment_concerns, notifications and admin_notifications
     tables, to admin/appointment_concern.php, to admin/no_show.php, to the
     automatic marking in config.php:207-268 and to sendClinicGmail().

  7. Reporting and prediction. Generate reports and Generate AI demand forecast
     map to admin/reports.php and to admin/demand_forecast.php, whose
     linearRegression() at line 51 is the prediction model of the study.

RELATIONSHIP TO THE OTHER DIAGRAMS
  The three figures describe what each actor is able to do, and the other
  diagrams of chapter 3 describe how that work is carried out and what it
  changes. Figure 12, the entity relationship diagram, holds the entities that
  the use cases read and write: the registration use cases of Figures 13 and 15
  insert into the users and the patients entities, the booking use cases of
  Figures 13 and 15 insert into the appointments entity, the queue use cases of
  Figures 14 and 15 write the queue entity, and the concern, notification and
  settings use cases write the corresponding entities of the same diagram. The
  data flow diagram of Figure 5 and Figure 6 shows the same functions as
  processes with the flows that carry data between them, the activity diagrams
  of Figures 8 to 11 show the same functions as ordered actions with their
  decisions, and the use case diagrams of Figures 13 to 15 show the same
  functions as the capabilities of the actors. The status values that the use
  cases produce, namely Pending, Confirmed, Completed, Cancelled and No-Show
  for an appointment and Waiting, Serving and Completed for a queue entry, are
  the values documented for those columns in the Data Dictionary of Section
  3.9.
