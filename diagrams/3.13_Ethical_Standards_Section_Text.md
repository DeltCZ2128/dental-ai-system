## 3.13 Ethical Standards and Considerations

The conduct of this study and the operation of the developed system were
guided by the ethical standards required of student research in the College of
Computer Studies, and by the requirements of Republic Act No. 10173, the Data
Privacy Act of 2012. Because the system handles the personal information of
patients and because the information gathered during the study comes from
human respondents, the researchers observed the standards summarized in Table
21. These standards cover the proper treatment of the respondents during data
gathering, the protection of the personal data held by the system, the limits
of what the system is permitted to do, and the honesty required in reporting
the results of the study.

### 3.13.1 Ethical Standards in the Conduct of the Study

Before any data were gathered, the researchers requested permission from the
management of Abalos Dental Clinic to conduct the study within the clinic and
to observe its existing appointment process. The purpose of the study, the
information to be collected, and the intended use of that information were
explained to the clinic in advance, and the activities carried out in the
clinic were conducted under the supervision of the project adviser.

The respondents of the evaluation described in Section 3.12 were informed of
the purpose of the evaluation and of what would be asked of them before the
questionnaire was administered. Participation was **voluntary**. No respondent
was pressured, required, or paid to take part, and no respondent was denied
service or placed at a disadvantage for declining to answer. Respondents were
told that they could stop answering the questionnaire at any time and that
they could decline any item they did not wish to answer, without giving a
reason. Each respondent was also given an **instructions to respondents** page,
which states what each point of the rating scale means and what each part of
the questionnaire measures, before the questionnaire was answered.

The identity of the respondents was kept **confidential**. The questionnaires
were answered without names, and the responses were tallied and reported as
grouped scores and weighted means, so that no individual answer can be traced
back to a particular respondent. No respondent is named anywhere in this
study, and no respondent's answer is presented on its own. Only the
researchers and the adviser had access to the accomplished questionnaires, and
the raw forms are kept by the researchers for the period required by the
institution and are not reproduced in this document.

The researchers likewise observed **honesty and integrity** in the treatment
of the data gathered. No response, score, or result reported in Chapter 4 was
invented, altered, or estimated. The evaluation figures reported in Sections
4.2 and 4.3 were computed only from the accomplished questionnaires actually
returned by the respondents, and any item that was left unanswered was handled
as a missing response rather than being filled in. The ideas, data, and
materials taken from other authors are acknowledged, and the sources used in
this study are listed in the References in accordance with the required
citation style. The researchers also declare that they have no financial or
personal interest in the development of the system beyond the completion of
this academic requirement.

### 3.13.2 Data Privacy, Confidentiality and Security

The patient records stored in the system constitute **personal information**
and, where they relate to the health of a patient, **sensitive personal
information** under the Data Privacy Act of 2012. The researchers therefore
applied the principles of transparency, legitimate purpose, and proportionality
to the data held in the system: only the information needed for scheduling
appointments and for maintaining patient records is collected; no information
is collected for a purpose unrelated to dental appointment management; and no
information is kept longer than the clinic requires it.

The system limits who can reach the data and what each user can do with it.
Access requires a **username and password**, and the passwords are stored as
one-way hashes, so the original password cannot be read from the database.
Each account carries a **role**, and the functions available to a user are
restricted to the role of that account: a patient reaches only the patient
functions, a dentist reaches the schedule and queue functions assigned to a
dentist, and the administrative functions are confined to the administrator.
The appointment information of a patient is therefore not reachable by another
patient. Database access is made through **parameterized queries**, and
information is **encoded before it is displayed** in the interface, which
protects the stored records against alteration or disclosure through the input
fields of the system.

The historical appointment records that feed the demand forecast of Section
3.10 are used only in the form of **daily appointment counts**. The forecast is
computed from the number of appointments recorded per day and does not use the
identity, name, address, contact number, or dental history of any patient, so
the forecast cannot reveal the appointment of an identifiable individual. Its
results are shown to the clinic as expected volume and as low, medium, or high
demand levels, and never as information about a particular patient.

The data gathered for the study itself were used only for the purposes
declared to the respondents and to the clinic. No patient record and no
respondent answer was copied into this document, published, posted online, or
shared with any party outside the study, and none of the data was used to
train, test, or demonstrate any model other than the appointment demand
forecast described in this chapter.

### 3.13.3 Ethical Limits of the System

The system was deliberately limited to the functions that do not require the
exercise of clinical judgement, in keeping with the delimitation stated in
Section 1.5. The system does **not** diagnose a dental condition, does **not**
recommend or plan a treatment, does **not** issue a prescription, and does
**not** replace the decision of a licensed dentist. The medical records stored
in the system are the records that the clinic itself writes; the system keeps
them and retrieves them, but it does not interpret them and it draws no
conclusion from them.

The demand forecast produced by the Linear Regression model of Section 3.10 is
**advisory information only**. It estimates the expected number of
appointments for the following seven days so that the clinic can prepare
schedules and resources, and it is presented to the administrator as expected
volume and as a low, medium, or high demand level. The forecast does not
confirm, reject, prioritise, or cancel any appointment, does not decide which
patient is served, and does not by itself change any record in the system.
Every appointment continues to be created, validated, confirmed, rescheduled,
or cancelled according to the rules described in this chapter and to the
decision of the clinic personnel, and the forecast is used only as one of the
considerations in planning.

The system likewise contains no function for online payment, billing,
accounting, insurance processing, or financial management, as stated in
Section 1.5, so no financial information of any patient is collected or
stored. The system is intended for the use of a single clinic, Abalos Dental
Clinic, and is not offered as a service to other clinics or to the public.

### 3.13.4 Limitations, Ownership and Use of Data

The researchers acknowledge the limits of the study and of the system. The
evaluation reported in Chapter 4 measures the acceptability of the system
against the six ISO/IEC 25010 characteristics of Table 18 as perceived by the
respondents of Section 3.12; it is not a laboratory measurement of performance
and it does not establish that the system will behave identically in every
clinic or under every workload. The demand forecast was tested against the
historical appointment records of one clinic, so its accuracy is described for
that clinic and that period only, and the researchers do not claim that the
same model generalizes to other clinics without retraining.

The appointment records, patient records, and all other clinic information
entered into the system remain the property of **Abalos Dental Clinic**. The
researchers claim no ownership over these records, and the clinic retains the
right to use, export, restrict, or delete its own data at any time. The
developed system is an academic prototype submitted in partial fulfillment of
the requirements of the degree, and no commercial claim is made over the
clinic data it was demonstrated with. Upon completion of the study, access to
the working copy of the data used for demonstration remains with the clinic or
is removed in accordance with the instruction of the institution and of the
clinic.

### Verification notes (not for submission)

Every technical claim in Section 3.13.2 was checked against the source code of
the system before it was written, and only controls that are actually present
are stated:

| Claim made in 3.13.2 | Evidence in the code |
|---|---|
| Passwords are stored as one-way hashes | `password_hash()` (12 call sites) and `password_verify()` (4 call sites), for example `login.php` line 64 |
| Access is limited by the role of the account | `$_SESSION['role']` set at login and role checks across 41 PHP files (75 references) |
| Database is reached through parameterized queries | `->prepare()` used 225 times; `->query()` and `->exec()` are never used with input |
| Information is encoded before display | `htmlspecialchars()` used 66 times, including the helper `e()` in `config.php` |

**Two claims are deliberately absent** because the code does not support them,
so do not add them to this section:

- there is no statement that the data are encrypted in transit, because the
  system runs over plain HTTP on the local XAMPP server, and
- there is no statement that sessions are hardened against session fixation,
  because `session_regenerate_id()` is never called.

**Before submission, two items should be corrected in the code rather than
disclosed in the chapter:**

1. **A live Gmail App Password is hardcoded in `config.php`.** The lines
   `define('CLINIC_SMTP_USER', ...)` and `define('CLINIC_SMTP_PASS', ...)` hold
   a real credential for the clinic mailbox that sends appointment reminders,
   even though the comment above them says never to commit the real password.
   The App Password should be revoked and regenerated in the Google account,
   and the value should be read from an environment variable or from a local
   file that is excluded from what is submitted. Until this is done, the
   confidentiality claim of Section 3.13.2 is contradicted by the source code.
2. **CSRF tokens are absent.** Forms in the administrative pages carry no
   anti-forgery token, so a logged-in administrator could be made to submit a
   form from another page.
